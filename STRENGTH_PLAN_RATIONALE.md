## Revision 2.3 — Decisions, 2026-09-10

Made after reviewing the first four sessions of the block (25 Aug, 27 Aug, 1 Sep, 8 Sep) against the 6,412-row Strong export.

### The hip thrust is permanently dead — and not replaced

Removed in 2.2 for an equipment reason. 2.3 confirms it should stay gone for a *programming* reason, so it never gets reconsidered:

- **The glutes already get 14 sets a week** where they are a prime or strong secondary mover — squat (4), BSS (6), RDL (4) — all loading the glute at long muscle length.
- **Plotkin et al. 2023** compared hip thrust against back squat head-to-head over 9 weeks: similar gluteus hypertrophy from both, with the squat clearly better for quads. The lift he cannot set up is not buying something the lifts he can set up are missing. Its one distinguishing feature is peak tension at *short* muscle length, the region with the weaker hypertrophy evidence.
- **Quads and calves are the goal.** Glutes are not.
- **Session A is already at his 90-minute ceiling.** Three added sets is 7–10 min he does not have.

**Action:** delete it from the Strong template so it stops appearing as a skipped exercise every week.

### The back extension was not doing the hip thrust's job

It was the 2.2 replacement. Three sessions say it is not working:

| Date | Load | Reps | RPE | Note |
|------|------|------|-----|------|
| 25 Aug | BW | 15 / 15 / 15 | 7 / 8 / 7 | |
| 1 Sep | BW | 15, 8, — | 6 | *"Just don't feel anything"* — abandoned mid-exercise |
| 8 Sep | 5 kg | 15 / 15 / 15 | 6 / 7 / 7 | |

Top of the rep range on every completed set, three sessions running, at RPE 6–7 against a target of 8 — and only 5 kg added against a plan that said start at 10 kg.

**Diagnosis: it is simply under-loaded.** He has a dedicated back extension unit in the garage, so the range is not truncated and the setup is not the problem — this was briefly mis-diagnosed as a bench-height issue on 2026-09-10 before he corrected it. A bodyweight back extension for 15 reps is an easy set for anyone, and 5 kg across three sessions has not changed that.

**Fix:** go to 10 kg, add 2.5 kg whenever all three sets reach 15 reps, and keep going. There is a lot of runway here — this lift should be carrying 20 kg+ by November. If RPE still reads low at a load that feels heavy to hold, add a 3 s eccentric and a pause at the top before adding more plate.

**Fallback if it still reads RPE 6 once it is genuinely loaded:** swap to a **single-leg dumbbell hip thrust** — shoulders on a bench, one foot planted, one dumbbell on the hip crease over a folded towel. No bar to get under, and single-leg halves the load requirement (16 kg in one hand does what 32.5 kg on a bar was doing). The cost is losing the only direct erector work, which matters given the back is the RDL limiter — so only make that trade if the back extension has genuinely proved dead.

### Calf load moves off the hands and into a rucksack

He is at 11 kg and climbing ~1.5 kg per session — 15–17 kg by early October. Revision 2.1 deliberately moved calf raises off the barbell partly to take a loaded handle out of the left hand while the thumb issue is unresolved, and a dumbbell has quietly reappeared. Switching to a plate-loaded rucksack now, while the load is small, costs nothing and has months more runway than a dumbbell does. See the left-hand investigation notes.

### Seated calf raise dropped

Two separate problems:

1. **He was running a version the plan retired.** The log reads `Seated Calf Raise (Plate Loaded)` at 40 kg, note *"Not practical in home gym, barbell wobbly on knees and don't feel much."* Revision 2.1 had already moved this to a dumbbell on a towel, precisely because a bar rolls.
2. **Even done correctly it is the lowest-value two sets in the plan.** Kinoshita et al. 2023 (12 weeks, within-subject contralateral): standing produced 9.2–12.4% gastrocnemius growth vs 0.6–1.7% seated, and **soleus grew equally in both** (2.1% vs 2.9%, n.s.). Standing already covers the soleus. Seated was kept only as cheap bolt-on volume, and it turned out not to be cheap.

**Weekly calf volume stays at 10** by moving those two sets into Thursday's standing calf raise: 3/leg → 5/leg. All 10 calf sets are now standing, in the better-evidenced position, with no setup change.

*Optional, if a bent-knee exposure is ever wanted again:* single-leg seated, one dumbbell stood vertically on the distal thigh, ball of the foot on a plate. One leg halves the load requirement and a vertical dumbbell cannot roll.

### Weighted decline sit-up dropped, not replaced

No decline bench exists in the home gym. It is also the weakest of the three core patterns for this athlete:

| Pattern | Exercise | Verdict |
|---------|----------|---------|
| Anti-extension | Ab wheel | Keep — the most transferable; it is what stops position collapsing in hour five |
| Anti-rotation | Pallof | Keep — free, once supersetted properly |
| Loaded spinal flexion | Decline sit-up | **Drop** |

Repeated loaded spinal flexion is the one core pattern with a genuine downside here — back pain logged 2026-04-28, *"feel it in lower back by end"* (3 Jul), *"back felt like limiting factor"* (20 Aug). It is also the pattern least related to riding, where the trunk's job is resisting movement rather than producing flexion. The core rationale is graded **C** throughout and does not claim to raise power.

Weekly core goes 10 → 8 sets, entirely in the two patterns that matter.

*If the slot is ever wanted back:* lying leg raise / reverse crunch on the floor — heels to the ceiling, lower slowly, lower back pressed into the floor. Progress by tempo, then straighter legs, then a light dumbbell between the feet. Loadable and progressable, far less spinal compression.

### Thursday squat recalibrated back to 35 kg

He has not been doing a narrow-stance squat. He has been doing Tuesday's shoulder-width squat at a lower load for higher reps — because that is what his Strong template said. So the 27 Aug reading of 35 kg × 12 at RPE 6 describes a *shoulder-width* squat 12.5 kg below his Tuesday working weight, not a narrow squat that was too light.

When he switches to an actual narrow stance it will get harder at the same load. **Do not take a raised load into a stance never squatted before.** Hold 35 kg, treat it as the week-1 calibration that never happened, and find the load giving 12 hard reps at RPE 9 — expect 35–40 kg.

### The delivery problem: the plan does not reach the gym

Both the superset and the narrow stance were stated plainly in the plan doc — `3a`/`3b` numbering plus an explicit paragraph, and a whole section on stance width with its own starting-load note. Neither was executed, because **he trains from the Strong template, not from a markdown file, and the template cannot carry either instruction**:

- Both squats log as plain `Squat (Barbell)`. Nothing distinguishes Tuesday's shoulder-width from Thursday's narrow. The app said they were the same lift, so they were run the same. This also corrupts the log — Strong's own progression tracking has been comparing 47.5 × 8 against 35 × 12 as if they were one lift.
- Strong has no concept of `3a`/`3b`. In the template it is exercise 3 and exercise 4 in order, so doing Pallof at the end of the session is the only available reading.

**This is a plan-delivery failure, not an adherence failure.** The fix is one-off and lives in Strong, not in the doc:

1. Create `Squat (Barbell) — Narrow` as a **separate custom exercise** for Thursday.
2. Rename the Pallof entry to `Pallof Press (in calf rest)` and drag it directly beneath the calf raise.
3. Any cue that must survive into the session goes **in the exercise name**. That is the only place in Strong where a cue is visible mid-set.

Same category as the minimal intervals.icu descriptions: rationale belongs in chat and in this document; the *execution cue* has to live in the tool.

## Session Duration — the model, and why it no longer applies

Revision 1 costed sessions at a flat 3.4 min/set. Across 21 logged sessions, minutes-per-set correlates with set count at **r = −0.72** — big sessions are more time-efficient per set. Fitting duration against set count instead:

> **minutes ≈ 26 + 2.2 × sets**  (R² = 0.77, halves the residual error of the flat model)

Two consequences: there is ~26 min of fixed overhead in every session regardless of size, and a marginal set costs 2.2 min rather than 3.4.

**But the block data has overtaken the model.** Strength A has run 90 / 87 / 91 min at 21 / 21 / 18 working sets. Fewer sets, same duration. Bottom-up, the 8 Sep session should have taken ~65 min; it took 91. **There is ~25 minutes of slack that is not warm-up, not work, and not scheduled rest**, and three sessions of advice about rest-timer discipline have not moved it.

**The operative conclusion for revision 2.3:** treat 90 minutes as fixed. Cutting an exercise from Strength A will not shorten it, so do not cut things to buy time — cut them only when they have stopped earning their place. Conversely, nothing can be *added* to Strength A, which is the decisive argument against replacing the hip thrust.

Where the overhead can genuinely be attacked, if it is ever worth trying again:
- Lay out plates, bench, step, band and rucksack before the first set.
- 3 ramp sets on the day's first barbell lift only.
- Log the set *during* the rest, not after — Strong's rest timer starts on save.

## Nordic Curls — REMOVED 2026-09-21

Two attempts (10 Sep, 17 Sep) and the lift was never set up: *"I just can't set up the Nordic curls in my home gym."* Slider leg curl and dumbbell prone leg curl were both offered as substitutes on 20 Sep and both rejected — *"a massive hassle to set up"*, and the dumbbell could not be picked up with the feet at all.

**Removed, not replaced.** A dumbbell single-leg RDL was written in on 2026-09-21 and withdrawn the same day under challenge. The justification did not survive scrutiny:

- **No evidence distinguishes a unilateral from a bilateral hinge for hypertrophy.** Bourne et al. 2017 separates hip extension from knee flexion; both RDL variants are hip extension, so citing it there was a misuse of the paper. The exercise-variation literature (Fonseca 2014) is one small study and does not carry the claim either.
- **Its own stop rule defeated it.** "Stop the set on balance, not on hamstring failure" concedes the set ends before the target muscle is challenged — and proximity to failure is among the better-supported hypertrophy variables. Placed third, after four heavy RDL sets and four narrow squats, the stabiliser limit would only tighten.
- **It failed the test that removed the good morning from this plan** — "redundant after RDLs". Same joint action, same muscle, same length profile.

Loading was *not* the objection: barbell RDL sits at 40 kg × 6 and the dumbbells reach 32 kg, so per-leg headroom existed for most of the block. The lift was dropped on stimulus quality, not on load.

**What was done instead:** nothing added on Thursday, the session shortened to ≈ 65 min, and the Tuesday back extension flagged for the loading it has always been short of. Direct hamstring volume is 4 sets/week against a 6–7 target. Closing that gap at home requires either more spinal loading — rejected, since the erectors already limit lift 1 — or equipment.

**Reopen this if a plate-loaded leg curl attachment is bought** — 72 sets of Lying Leg Curl (Machine) are logged between 2019 and 2022, so the lift is known and needs no setup.

### Original introduction protocol (superseded)

First-ever exposure is **Thursday 10 Sep**. Nordics produce the most severe eccentric-onset soreness of any common hamstring exercise, routinely 4–7 days in the untrained. Revision 1 had the first exposure nine days before the first 100-miler at TSB −19; revision 2 deferred it past the event.

## Heel Elevation

He squats with a plate under his heels on Tuesday and always has. Nothing in the 5,745 logged rows recorded that, so revisions 1 and 2 were written assuming Tuesday was flat and Thursday's heel elevation was what made the days different. It was not — both days were the same movement at the same joint angles, separated only by rep range. **Stance width, not elevation, is what differentiates them.**

**How much elevation.** What he already uses is right: roughly **2–4 cm, about 15–25°**. Past ~25° you stop buying knee travel and start unloading the posterior chain and stacking shear through the knee, which matters given the knee history. **Do not raise Thursday's elevation to differentiate the days.**

**Keep elevating.** Depth beats purity — deep squats build measurably more quad than shallow ones, so using a plate to reach depth is a straight win over a flat squat that cannot be bottomed out.

**The ankle problem should shrink over this block.** The calf raises carry a 2 s pause at the bottom stretch — loaded stretching of the plantarflexors at long muscle length, the intervention with the best evidence for increasing dorsiflexion range. Test every three or four weeks: squat one warm-up set flat and see how depth feels.

## Evidence

**A** = multiple trials or meta-analysis, consistent. **B** = single good trial or strong mechanism. **C** = reasoned from principle, thin direct evidence.

| The plan does this | Because | Grade |
|--------------------|---------|-------|
| Trains quads and calves twice weekly | 2×/week beats 1× at matched weekly volume — Schoenfeld, Ogborn & Krieger 2016. Pelland et al. 2024 finds frequency's independent effect is small once volume is equated, so the real argument is that 2× is how the volume fits. | A |
| ~11 quad sets/week, 10 calf sets/week | Hypertrophy keeps rising with weekly sets across the studied range — Pelland et al. 2024 estimates ~0.24%/set around 12 sets/week with no clear plateau. | A |
| Heavy 6–8s alongside 10–15s | Growth is similar from ~6 to ~35 reps when sets are taken near failure — Schoenfeld et al. 2021. The heavy work buys strength at no hypertrophy cost. | A |
| RPE 8–9 rather than failure | Refalo et al. 2023: closer to failure is *slightly* better (ES ~0.15–0.21 across 0–5 RIR), steep drop-off only beyond 4–5 RIR. At 1–2 RIR nearly all of it is captured at much lower fatigue cost — which is also why the no-spotter squat cap costs little. | A |
| Full-depth squats | Deep squats produce greater quad hypertrophy than shallow — Bloomquist et al. 2013; Kubo et al. 2019. | A |
| Paused stretch at the bottom of calf raises | Training at long muscle lengths drives greater triceps surae growth — Kassiano et al. 2023. | A |
| **All 10 calf sets standing, none seated** | Kinoshita et al. 2023 (*Front Physiol*, 12 weeks, within-subject contralateral): standing produced 9.2–12.4% gastrocnemius growth vs 0.6–1.7% seated, and **soleus grew equally in both** (2.1% vs 2.9%, n.s.). Revision 1's rationale — that seated is needed because standing "barely touches" the soleus — is contradicted by the best direct evidence. Caveat: n = 14, untrained. | B |
| **No hip thrust or direct short-length glute work** | Plotkin et al. 2023: hip thrust and back squat produced similar gluteus hypertrophy over 9 weeks, with the squat clearly superior for quads. With 14 weekly sets already loading the glute at long muscle length, the hip thrust adds little that squats, BSS and RDLs do not. | B |
| Upright torso, not a longer stride, for quad bias on split squats | Longer split-squat step lengths raise hip-extensor activation with limited effect on the knee extensors (step-length EMG studies, 2023). Caveat: EMG, not hypertrophy outcomes. | B |
| ~~RDLs *and* Nordics~~ — **no longer applies** | Bourne et al. 2017 stands as evidence; the plan no longer acts on it. With the Nordic gone there is no knee-flexion work, so the distal hamstring and the biceps femoris short head (which crosses only the knee) are untrained. Accepted as a bounded cost: on the bike the hamstring acts as a hip extensor, and knee-flexion strength matters most for sprint mechanics and strain prevention. The loss is to the leg-mass goal, not to cycling. | A |
| Nordics deferred, then ramped | Nordic eccentric DOMS is severe and long-lasting in the unaccustomed. | B |
| Strength training alongside cycling at all | Concurrent strength training improves cycling economy and performance — Rønnestad & Mujika 2014. | A |
| 6+ hours between AM lifting and a PM ride | Separation preserves both adaptations — Robineau et al. 2016. | A |
| 1.6–2.0 g/kg protein | ~1.6 g/kg is where resistance-training gains plateau; CI extends to ~2.2 — Morton et al. 2018. | A |
| A scheduled deload at week 8 | Standard practice; direct evidence for *planned* vs *reactive* deloads is genuinely thin. Included because the cost is two sessions and the alternative is discovering it was needed. | C |
| Core trained for endurance and position, not power | Trunk muscles work on the bike by resisting movement and holding position rather than producing force. Judge it by how the back feels in hour five, not by watts. | C |
| **Loaded spinal flexion removed** | Anti-extension and anti-rotation are the transferable patterns for holding position on a bike; loaded repeated flexion is the one with a plausible downside given the logged back history. Reasoned, not trialled. | C |
| Core supersetted | Robbins et al. 2010 covers **agonist–antagonist** pairs, not two trunk exercises. Calf raise + Pallof genuinely do not compete, so that pairing is free; the honest saving on a compound pair is 2–3 min. | C |

**What is deliberately not claimed:** that core work will fix hand pressure; that this plan will raise FTP; that 2 kg of leg mass is compatible with 4 W/kg at 68 kg. The first is a fit problem, the second is not what a hypertrophy block does, and the third is a genuine trade-off being chosen while off-block.

## Hand Pressure — Fit Checks

Not a training adaptation and not something the gym work will move. Ranked by how much they matter:

| Check | What to look for |
|-------|------------------|
| **1. Saddle tilt** | Highest-yield check. Nose-down slides you forward and dumps weight onto the hands continuously. Level, or a degree nose-up, is typical. |
| **2. Saddle fore/aft** | Too far forward shifts weight ahead of the bottom bracket and onto the bars. |
| **3. Reach** | A **+10 mm stem from the January fit** is on record. More reach, more forward lean, more weight through the hands. If the pain started after January, first suspect. |
| **4. Saddle change** | The **April re-fit fitted a new saddle**. A different shape or rail position resets effective tilt and setback even if the post did not move. |
| **5. Hand position** | Change position every 10–15 minutes. Sustained pressure in one spot causes the trouble, not total load. |
| **6. Contact points** | Padded gloves and thicker bar tape are cheap and immediate. |

**Worth not ignoring:** numbness or tingling lingering for hours after riding is nerve compression rather than fatigue, and is worth getting assessed rather than trained around.

> **One acknowledged gap.** The plan is scoped to lower body and core. The cost is that holding the torso off the bars for six hours is partly a scapular and upper-back endurance task, and nothing here trains it. If hand pressure persists after the fit checks, a single set of rows or face pulls twice a week is the cheapest thing that addresses the part core work cannot.
